# Optics

## Refraction and Snell's law
Light bends towards the normal when it slows down, and away from the normal
when it speeds up. The refractive index of a material is

  n = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in the material

- Snell's law: n1 sin(i) = n2 sin(r), with the angles measured from the normal.
- Air is taken as n = 1.00; water is 1.33, crown glass about 1.52.
- The frequency never changes across a boundary, so the wavelength shortens by
  the factor n.

## Total internal reflection
Inside a dense medium, light hitting the boundary beyond the critical angle is
reflected instead of refracted.

Q: What is the critical angle and when does it exist?
A: The angle of incidence inside the dense medium for which the refracted ray
would leave along the boundary at 90 degrees, so sin(c) = n2 / n1. It only
exists when light travels from a higher to a lower refractive index, for
example glass to air.

- Uses: optical fibres, where a core of higher index is clad with a lower index
  so the light is guided along with almost no loss; and prisms in binoculars,
  which reflect better than a silvered mirror.

## Lenses
- A converging lens is thicker in the middle; a diverging lens is thinner.
- The thin lens equation: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u with the real-is-positive convention,
  and magnification m = v / u.
- A virtual image is formed when the object is inside the focal length of a
  converging lens; the image is upright and cannot be projected on a screen.
- Power is measured in dioptres, P = 1 / f with f in metres.

## Practical reminders
- Read the scale with the eye level with the mark to avoid parallax error.
- Draw ray diagrams with a ruler, and mark the principal focus on both sides.
- When measuring a focal length by a distant object, use something far away such
  as a window frame across a room, so the rays arrive almost parallel.
